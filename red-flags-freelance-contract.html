Red Flags in a Freelance Contract: 8 Things to Check Before You Sign

A contract can look fine at first glance and still leave you unpaid, overworked, or on the hook for problems you didn't cause. Here are eight warning signs to look for before you sign.

1. The work isn't clearly described

If the contract says something like "marketing help" or "website work" without listing exactly what you'll deliver, the client can keep asking for more. A good contract spells out the deliverables, so both sides know when the job is finished.

2. No clear payment terms

Look for the amount, the due date, and how you'll be paid. If the contract doesn't say when payment is due, or says payment happens "upon client satisfaction," you may wait a long time to get paid.

3. Very long payment windows

Terms like "net 60" or "net 90" mean the client has 60 or 90 days after your invoice to pay. That can be normal for big companies, but it's a lot for a small freelancer to carry. Know the timeline before you agree to it.

4. Unlimited revisions

If the contract doesn't limit how many rounds of changes are included, one project can turn into endless free work. A set number of revisions, with extra rounds billed separately, protects your time.

5. You give up ownership before you're paid

Some contracts hand the client full rights to your work the moment you deliver it. It's safer when ownership transfers only after you've been paid in full.

6. Only one side can cancel

Check who can end the contract and what happens when they do. If the client can cancel at any time without paying for work already done, that's a risk to you.

7. An overly broad non-compete

A clause that stops you from working for "any company in the industry" for years can block your income. Watch for limits on who you can work for, where, and for how long.

8. You're responsible for everything

Words like "indemnify" or "hold harmless" can make you pay for losses, lawsuits, or problems you didn't cause. Make sure any responsibility you take on is limited and fair.
